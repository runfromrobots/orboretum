Education & Events

Learning at Root Arboretum

Root Arboretum is designed as an outdoor classroom and research site. We offer field study opportunities aligned with NY State science and social studies standards, covering native ecology, watershed health, forest succession, and the region's layered history.

School Field Trips

We welcome K–12 groups. Programs are customized to grade level and curriculum needs:

  • Forest Ecology & Native Plants: Explore the structure and biodiversity of northeastern hardwood forests; learn about native species and their ecological roles.
  • Watershed & Water Quality: Investigate how the property's position in the Yatesville Creek watershed connects to the Mohawk River system.
  • Forest Succession: Observe and interpret different forest age classes and successional stages.
  • Local & Regional History: Discover four layers of history—from pre-colonial Mohawk territory to Revolutionary War events to mid-century horticulture to modern conservation.

Contact us → to arrange a visit or discuss custom programming.

Research & Partnerships

Root Arboretum welcomes research collaborations with university faculty, graduate students, ecologists, foresters, and historians. Our documented living collection and diverse forest ecosystems offer rich opportunities for study.

Areas of research interest include:

  • Forest ecology and successional dynamics
  • Native plant ecology and regional restoration
  • Heritage plant history and horticulture
  • Watershed health and riparian function
  • Indigenous plant knowledge and Haudenosaunee history

Interested researchers should reach out with a brief description of your work.

Public Events & Interpretive Programming

Root Arboretum hosts at least one public tree-related event annually (part of our ArbNet Level I accreditation commitment). Programming may include:

  • Arbor Day celebrations: Tree planting and interpretive walks
  • Seasonal guided walks: Spring wildflowers, fall foliage, winter forest structure
  • Educational workshops: Native plant identification, forest ecology, local history
  • Community restoration days: Trail maintenance, seed collection, propagation
